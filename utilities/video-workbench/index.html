Methods, interpretation & research notes

What the measurements mean

Lip aperture (landmarks 13–14) and width (61–291) are pixel distances divided by outer-eye distance (33–263). Lower lip (14) and jaw (152) positions use an eye-centered, roll-corrected 2D frame. Image width and height are accounted for. Optional outer-eye calibration provides approximate image-plane millimeters. Raw depth remains model-relative.

Range is max minus min. Speed uses absolute first differences in seconds and excludes detection gaps. No temporal filter is applied; noise affects speed. Coordination is zero-lag Pearson correlation with at least three valid pairs, and is unavailable for constant signals. Correlation with audio uses the entire mixed audio track and does not identify the speaker.

Shoulder tilt and torso lean are image-plane angles using joints with visibility ≥ 0.6. Expression coefficients (0–1) describe modeled facial motion; they are not emotion diagnoses or calibrated FACS action units. Asymmetry is the absolute left–right difference.

Research context

Sanchez et al., From Lab to Laptop: Validating 3D Speech Kinematics with MediaPipe Face Mesh (Interspeech 2026, supplied manuscript), motivated the speech measurements. Its validation used participant calibration, rigid 3D head correction, matched filtering, and optical motion capture in four adults. This workbench uses a simpler exploratory 2D method and does not inherit the study’s millimeter-scale accuracy claims.

Schulze et al. (2025) study landmark selection for speech exercises. Li et al. (2025) investigate learned AU–blendshape representations.

Built with MediaPipe Face Landmarker and Pose Landmarker, runtime 0.10.32, float16 model revision 1. Model/WASM files load from Google Storage and jsDelivr when analysis starts. Once cached by your browser they may be reused; first use requires internet access.

Sequential sampling may be below the source frame rate. Tracks use geometric proximity and velocity, with a new segment after ambiguity or an absence over 0.8 s. Lighting, occlusion, perspective and crossings affect estimates. Review the overlays before interpreting results.
